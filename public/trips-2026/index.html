Where to stay

Friday · best base

Port Angeles

Best for Friday’s late Hurricane Ridge window and an early US-101 start Saturday.

  • Choose the west side of town or near US-101 to save morning time.
  • Most dinner, fuel, and grocery flexibility.
  • About 30–40 minutes to Lake Crescent.
Saturday · 3-day plan

Forks

Best launch point for the Hoh/coast day and Sunday’s return toward Sol Duc.

  • Practical, central, and easier for late coast return.
  • Fuel and food are available; evening choices narrow early.
  • Rialto is roughly 20–25 minutes away.
Quiet alternative

Lake Crescent corridor

Scenic and quiet, but less efficient for Hoh + coast and with fewer evening services.

  • Good if lake atmosphere matters more than maximum coverage.
  • Keep food and check-in times in mind.
  • Not the best choice for a Saturday-night Seattle return.

Sea stacks, open water, and zero-to-short walks

Scenic beaches & coastal stops

The first three are the best fit; the others are easy route swaps.

Best drama · primary

Rialto Beach

Rocky shore, giant drift logs, pounding surf, offshore islands, and sea stacks. It needs no destination hike: walk north only as far as daylight and water allow.

Parking-to-beach access · 60–120 min

Mora Road is closed through Oct 15; use only after NPS confirms reopening.

Easy scenic pairing

First Beach · La Push

Broad Pacific frontage and Quillayute Needles views right by La Push. Use it as a 20–30 minute coastal pull-off before Rialto.

Minimal walking · 20–45 min

This is Quileute Reservation land. Follow posted access/parking rules and respect the community.

Best sea stacks · fallback

Ruby Beach

A short forest descent opens onto Abbey Island, driftwood, and classic sea-stack scenery. This is the clean fallback if Rialto access is uncertain.

0.1 mi to beach · 60–90 min

The path is short but steep in places; beach-log conditions shift with storms.

Zero-hike overlook

Ediz Hook

A drive-out spit in Port Angeles with harbor, Strait of Juan de Fuca, and Olympic views. Ideal if Friday timing is too tight for a second walk.

Drive-up views · 15–30 min

Strait-side swap

Salt Creek & Crescent Bay

Rocky Tongue Point, open Strait views, sea cliffs, and a crescent of sand. Use it Sunday instead of Sol Duc when the coast is clear and the interior is wet.

Short paths / viewpoints · 45–90 min

Tide pools require a genuinely low tide; county gate hours are posted at the entrance.

Southern-loop option

Kalaloch + Tree of Life

Wide sandy coast, bluff views, and the famous suspended-root spruce. Add this only if you skip Sol Duc and return to Seattle through Olympia.

Short beach walks · adds substantial driving

Not recommended on the Saturday-return version.

Saturday tide reality: La Push low tide is about 11:03 am (~4.7 ft) and high tide about 4:52 pm (~7.1 ft). That makes the afternoon good for scenery, not tide pooling or rounding headlands. Stay on open beach, watch the ocean continuously, and keep off drift logs.

Bring in the car

Water, food, headlamps, rain shells, warm layers, dry socks/shoes, traction-friendly footwear, paper/offline map, power bank, first aid, and a full fuel tank. There is no potable water at Hurricane Ridge.
